The members of the Taskforce Governance and Sustainability and the editorial team have finalized the joint position paper 
["Status Quo and Future of NFDI - A Perspective of the Subject Consortia,"](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14277471) which 
has been approved by a clear majority of the consortia. DataPLANT was heavily involved in the central editorial team. 

As key players in building the National Research Data Infrastructure (NFDI), the 26 subject consortia present their first 
evaluation after four years of joint work. In the current phase of structural evaluation and the subsequent decision by 
the GWK regarding the sustainability of the NFDI, the consortia are providing their perspective on the status quo and the 
future of the NFDI. In parallel, the interested academic community, the committees of the Alliance of Scientific 
Organizations, and other bodies that critically and constructively accompany the development of the NFDI across subject 
boundaries are being informed. The paper contains two essential demands:

The NFDI is primarily an "investment in people" and fundamentally differs from traditional infrastructure projects that 
primarily invest in hardware. Over 750 dedicated and qualified professionals are driving the comprehensive coordination 
processes of the NFDI forward, developing services, providing practical advice to researchers, imparting data competencies, 
and coordinating national and international integrations. The success of the NFDI now crucially depends on creating 
attractive career prospects for this highly qualified personnel, a central goal that has so far been inadequately addressed 
structurally. To ensure a sustainable infrastructure, not only must new professional fields be established and recognized, 
but also a long-term funding structure is needed to replace the previous project funds. However, the subject consortia 
currently lack the perspective for personnel development beyond the year 2028, which is associated with significant risks 
of losing competencies.

Enabling the long-term operation of services: In the first four years since the establishment of the NFDI, the consolidation 
of a wide range of scientific resources has gained momentum, and the subject consortia are bringing forth joint services 
and continuously developing them. To consolidate previously complex or precarious organizational forms, operators must be 
able to rely on reliable infrastructures with planned long-term funding that also covers the additional effort for 
governance, development support, and IT security. Furthermore, many existing services require a legally secure framework 
that allows for reimbursement of costs when services are opened to a broader user base, for example, beyond one's own 
institution or network.
